CO₂ Cold Gas Thruster & Thrust Stand

A 1.5 N CO₂ cold-gas thruster and the flexure-pendulum thrust stand to test it, built to find out how closely a real nozzle matches isentropic theory.

The goal

Cold-gas thrusters are the simplest form of rocket propulsion, and they're still flown on spacecraft for fine attitude control. I wanted to design one from first principles, build it, and then measure how its thrust and specific impulse compare with what isentropic nozzle theory predicts.

How it works

  • Nozzle sizing: a parametric tool in Python, based on choked-flow relations for CO₂, sets the throat and exit geometry for the target thrust. The results feed the CAD model and the manufacturing drawings.
  • Thrust stand: a flexure pendulum turns thrust into a small, measurable deflection, read by an HX711 load cell with an ESP32 logging the data.
  • Pneumatics: a CO₂-compatible feed system specified to work at sub-zero temperatures, since the gas cools sharply as it expands.
  • Data reduction: a Python pipeline handles load-cell calibration and calculates thrust and impulse, with a full uncertainty analysis.
